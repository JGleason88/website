1 in 4 GTA homes just sold for less than the owner paid.

New data shows almost a quarter of GTA resales in the past year closed below the seller's purchase price. For people who bought in 2021 and 2022, it was 4 out of 5. Here's what's behind it, what it means up here in Simcoe, and what to do before you sell or renew.

By Joshua GleasonNovember 21, 20266 min read

The numbers

Valery, a Toronto real estate data firm, went through every GTA resale on TRREB's MLS® that closed between October 25, 2025 and October 24, 2026 where it could match the seller's original purchase price. Of 27,272 sales, 6,767 closed below what the owner originally paid. That's 24.8%.

And that's before real estate commission, legal fees and any mortgage penalty. The real hit for most of those sellers was bigger than the gap between the two prices.

Of the 4,587 sellers who bought in 2021 or 2022, 3,669 sold for less than they paid: 80%. Those were the years of bidding wars and near-record-low rates. People paid peak prices because the payment looked affordable at 2%. Prices have come down since, and the cheap money is gone.

Valery's Daniel Foch told the Financial Post that many owners can't afford to sell, so they'd rather "lose money slow than lose money fast." In other words, a lot of the 2021–22 crowd is holding on and hoping, not because the math works, but because selling makes the loss real.

What about Barrie and Simcoe?

This study is GTA-only, and Valery didn't look at Simcoe County. But our market followed the same curve. Barrie's average price briefly went above $800,000 at the 2022 peak. In September 2026, the average across Simcoe County was $768,850, down 2.9% from a year earlier, according to TRREB.

The honest read: if you bought in Barrie or around Simcoe in late 2021 or the first half of 2022, there's a real chance your home is worth about what you paid, or less, once you count the cost of selling. If you bought before 2020, you're very likely still well ahead. Don't guess, though. Get a real number for your street.

Bought in 2021 or 2022? Here's what to do

1. Don't sell in a panic

If you don't have to move, the cheapest option is usually to stay put. A loss on paper isn't a loss until you sell. Prices may keep sliding for a while, but if you can carry the home comfortably, time is on your side.

2. Plan your renewal now

If you took a 5-year fixed in 2021 or 2022, your renewal lands in 2026 or 2027, at higher rates than you signed at. That payment jump is what pushes a lot of people into selling when they didn't want to. Start shopping 4 to 6 months before your renewal date. Extending your amortization, switching lenders or consolidating other debt can lower the payment enough to let you ride it out.

3. If you have to sell, know your number first

Job change, separation, growing family: sometimes you have to move. Before you list, work out your real net: today's value, minus your mortgage balance, minus commission, legal fees and any prepayment penalty. Then look at whether you can port your mortgage to the next home and avoid the penalty altogether.

4. Buying? This is the other side of the trade

Every one of those 6,767 sales had a buyer who paid less than the last owner did. If you've been waiting on the sidelines, sellers who bought at the peak are often motivated and open to negotiating.

My take

This isn't a reason to panic. It's a reason to plan. The people who get hurt in a market like this are the ones forced to sell on someone else's timeline: a renewal they couldn't afford, a payment they didn't see coming.
