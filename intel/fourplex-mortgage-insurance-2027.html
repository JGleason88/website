Fourplexes just got a lot easier to finance.

From February 1, 2027, newly built triplexes and fourplexes can get insured financing of up to 90%, with no need to live there and no price cap. Here's how it works, and why it matters in Barrie.

By Josh GleasonNovember 15, 20266 min read

What changed

The 2026 federal budget, tabled November 5, changes the mortgage insurance rules for "missing middle" housing: buildings bigger than a house but smaller than an apartment tower. For newly built three- and four-unit homes, insured financing will be available:

  • Up to 90% of the property's value on completion. That means 10% down.
  • No owner-occupancy requirement. You don't have to live in one of the units. It can be a pure rental.
  • No maximum property value. The $1.5 million cap is removed for these properties.
  • Up to a 30-year amortization on the completed property.
  • Construction financing can be insured too.

Before and after

Until now, a fourplex was hard to finance as an investment. The 90% insured financing for 3 to 4 units was built for owner-occupiers. If you weren't going to live there, financing generally topped out at 80%, so you needed at least 20% down.

The budget's own example

A builder plans a $3 million fourplex to rent out all four units. Today, it's over the $1.5 million cap and wouldn't qualify for insurance. Under the new rules, the builder could get an insured loan of up to $2.7 million, which is 90%, amortized over up to 30 years.

At Barrie scale: on a new fourplex valued at $1.6 million when complete, 90% financing is a $1.44 million insured loan and $160,000 of your own money, instead of the $320,000 or more a 20% down payment would need. That's simple arithmetic, not a quote: your numbers depend on the build, the appraisal and the lender.

Why Barrie is set up for this

Financing was only half the problem. The other half was zoning, and Barrie has already dealt with that. City council approved a zoning by-law change allowing four residential units on residentially zoned lots, as of right: if your plan meets the zoning rules, you don't need a rezoning application to build it.

Four units allowed per lot plus 90% insured financing is the combination that makes small infill projects work. Expect more builders, investors and homeowners with large lots to start running the numbers in 2027.

The fine print

  • New builds only. This is for newly constructed triplexes and fourplexes, and purchases of newly completed ones. It is not a way to buy an existing fourplex with 10% down.
  • You still have to qualify. On a completed property, housing costs generally can't exceed 39% of gross income and total debt payments 44%, the stress test applies, and at least one borrower or guarantor needs a credit score of 600 or higher.
  • Construction loans are assessed differently. The usual amortization and debt-ratio limits don't apply during construction; insurers use their own criteria to assess whether you can repay.
  • Lenders choose whether to offer it. The rules start February 1, 2027, but each lender decides when, and whether, to offer the product.
  • Premiums and full criteria aren't published yet. Expect insurance premiums on top, and details that may change before launch.

What about existing multiplexes?

The current rules still apply to existing properties. As a general guide:

  • Living in one unit of a 3- or 4-unit home: insured financing is already available up to 90%.
  • Buying an existing 2 to 4 unit rental you won't live in: insured options are generally capped at 80%, so plan on 20% down.

Bigger buildings: 5 to 50 units

The budget also lets the private mortgage insurers cover residential buildings of 5 to 50 units, from the same date: up to 90% for new construction (85% otherwise) and amortizations of up to 50 years for new construction (40 otherwise), with net worth and rental income tests. That's a bigger-league product, but it's the same idea: more financing for small rental buildings.

My take

This is one of the most important changes for small investors in years. For the first time, a regular person with a good lot in Barrie can realistically build four rental units with 10% down and a 30-year amortization. It isn't easy money: you still have to qualify, the build has to pencil out, and rents have to cover the costs. But if you've got a lot with potential, or you've been thinking about building, now is the time to run the numbers so you're ready when lenders start taking applications on February 1.
