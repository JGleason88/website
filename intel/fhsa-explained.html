First-time buyers

The FHSA, explained in 90 seconds.

If you've never owned a home, this is the best savings account in Canada for your down payment. Here's everything you need to know.

By Josh GleasonNovember 9, 20262 min read

What it is

The First Home Savings Account (FHSA) is a registered account for saving toward your first home. It combines the best part of an RRSP with the best part of a TFSA:

  • Going in: contributions are tax-deductible, like an RRSP.
  • Coming out: withdrawals for your first home are tax-free, like a TFSA, and you never pay them back.

Who can open one

  • You're a resident of Canada, 18 or older and under 72.
  • You're a first-time buyer: you haven't lived in a home that you, or your spouse or common-law partner, owned at any point this year or in the previous four calendar years.

The rules that matter

  • Room only starts when you open it. You don't build up room before then, so open one now even if you put in $0.
  • Max $16,000 in one year. That's $8,000 for the year plus up to $8,000 carried forward from last year.
  • 15-year window. The account can stay open for up to 15 years, or until the end of the year you turn 71, whichever comes first.
  • Not buying after all? You can move the money into your RRSP tax-free, without using any RRSP room.

Stack it with the Home Buyers' Plan

You can use your FHSA and the RRSP Home Buyers' Plan for the same home. The Home Buyers' Plan lets you borrow up to $60,000 from your RRSP, which you repay over 15 years.

Up to $100,000 per person toward a down payment: $40,000 from your FHSA (plus growth) and $60,000 from your RRSP. A couple buying together can each do it.

My take

If you're even thinking about buying in the next few years, open an FHSA this month. The room only starts counting once it's open, the deduction lowers your tax bill, and if you change your mind, it rolls into your RRSP. There's very little downside.
